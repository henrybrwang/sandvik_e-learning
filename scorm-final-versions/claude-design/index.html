Claude Design

Claude Design is the visual Claude experience for exploring, drafting and refining design directions, prototypes, slides, one-pagers, layouts and graphics through chat, ready for human review. This module shows you when it is the right product, how its capabilities work, and the judgement that turns a polished generated draft into something Sandvik can rely on.

Claude can accelerate your work, but you remain responsible for what you enter, approve, share and publish. Follow Sandvik policy, protect sensitive information, and review outputs before relying on them.

Getting started

Claude Design opens from the Claude sidebar and runs in your browser. Once you have it, starting a design takes three steps: open it, optionally set a design system and template, then describe what you want and refine.

01
Open it from Products

In the Claude sidebar, open Design under Products, alongside Code, to start a new design.

02
Pick a design system and template

Both are optional. When an approved design system is available, selecting it keeps work on brand, and a template gives consistent structure; the Template selector defaults to None.

03
Describe what you want, then refine

Write a short brief in the prompt box, generate, then refine with mark up before any review.

How Design works

Claude Design is built for exploring before committing. Rather than producing one finished design, it helps you try a few directions, choose one, and refine it into a reviewable draft.

The whole flow runs in chat, so you stay in control at every step and nothing is final until the right owner reviews it.

The core loop

Each design moves through the same seven steps. Most of the work is in the middle: exploring directions and refining the one you choose.

1Write a brief: goal, audience, sources and constraints.
2Clarify: Claude asks you a couple of questions so you can make the right decisions.
3Explore two or three directions instead of one.
4Choose one direction to take forward.
5Refine it with mark up, pointing at elements to change.
6Review for brand, accessibility, accuracy and sensitive information.
7Share a reviewed design through an approved route.

Explore, then choose

Ask for a few distinct directions up front. Comparing them is faster than perfecting the first idea, and it usually leads to a better result.

Directions for a one-pager
Direction A
Bold headline, single key message.
Direction B
Step-by-step layout for a process.
Direction C
Data-led, with a simple summary chart.
Explore a few, then choose one to refine.

Tool capabilities · 1 of 4

Design systems

Brand you teach Claude

A design system teaches Claude your visual language, so generated work follows approved brand instead of an invented one. For Sandvik work, the Sandvik Design System is your default: select it for every new design so the output follows Sandvik's branding from the start.

Default
Always start from the Sandvik Design System. It is the default for Sandvik work. Choose a different design system only if you have a specific approved reason.
How it is created
By connecting Figma or GitHub, by uploading slides and assets, or by building from existing React components with Claude Code, which gives the highest fidelity.
Why it matters
Select the existing Sandvik Design System so generated work follows Sandvik's branding guidelines. It is the strongest brand-control lever in Claude Design and keeps layout, colour and typography on brand from the start.
Good habit: select the Sandvik Design System at the start, and still run the brand review. A design system improves consistency, but it does not replace Brand approval.
Tool capabilities · 2 of 4

Templates

Structure you start from

A template gives a new design a consistent starting structure and format. Claude Design shows a template selector that defaults to "None". Starting from an approved template keeps layouts predictable and reduces rework.

The standard templates
Prototype: a clickable concept that shows the flow and interaction of a screen before anything is built.
Slides: a presentation deck for a concept or an update.
Document: a text-led page such as a one-pager, brief or report.
Wireframe: a low-fidelity layout that focuses on structure, not finished visuals.
Animation: a short motion or animated concept.
How it helps
It keeps layout, sections and spacing consistent across designs, so you start from a structure instead of a blank page and spend the time on content rather than the frame.
What it controls
The starting layout, the sections that appear, and the overall format of the design.
Why it matters
The template selector defaults to None. Until an approved Sandvik template exists, a generated structure is a starting point, not an approved layout, so confirm it fits the audience and use.
Good habit: start from an approved template where one exists, instead of a blank page, and confirm the template fits the audience and use.
Tool capabilities · 3 of 4

Editing the output

Adjust the project

Each design Claude generates is a project. The first version is never the finished product. It is a starting point you adjust, and one you can share with other people. Use the toolbar to refine it: point at an element to mark up a change, leave comments, edit directly, toggle tweaks, or present it.

Editing makes refinement precise, but it does not approve anything. A project is still a draft until the right owner signs off.

Toolbar actions
Mark up
Point at an element and describe the change you want.
Comments
Leave notes on the design to resolve as you refine.
Edit
Adjust the content directly in the design.
Show / Hide tweaks
Toggle the adjustable controls on the design on or off.
Present
Walk through the design full-screen with someone.
Share
Open the sharing options to send the project to others (covered on the next page).
Good habit: use mark up to make feedback specific, then run the full review before sharing.
Tool capabilities · 4 of 4

Sharing

Send the project to others

A project can be shared with other people once it is ready. Sharing is fast, so review the project first, then share through an approved route. The Share button opens three ways to share.

Share link
Invite people by name or email and set Can edit or Can comment, or give Anyone at Sandvik with the link access, then copy the link. Sharing stays inside Sandvik.
Export
Download the project as a standalone PDF, editable PowerPoint (.pptx), a project archive (.zip) of every file in the project, or a self-contained HTML file.
Send to
Mainly used to send the project to Claude Code to continue the work there, for example to turn a prototype concept into a real implementation. A prototype is still not production-ready software.
Before you share, review the project for brand, accuracy and sensitive information, set the narrowest permission that works (Can comment rather than Can edit unless editing is needed), and use only approved Sandvik routes. For external or Sandvik-branded material, get Brand and Legal review.
Good habit: review first, share with the narrowest access that works, and never share a first draft as if it were approved.

Claude is approved for data classified i1–i3 under the Sandvik Information Classification System: i1 Public, i2 Restricted (the default for most documents) and i3 Confidential. Usage and cost

Visual exploration can involve many iterations, generated variants, large files, exports and repeated refinement. At Sandvik, Claude Design usage is direct and consumption-based: there are no five-hour session or weekly limits, but every action consumes tokens billed pay as you go to your cost center, so costs can rise quickly.

Iterating many variants with large files and repeated exports can use capacity quickly. Keep an eye on your usage and keep work scoped.

Practical habits
›Start with a clear brief.
›Explore a limited number of directions, then choose before deep refinement.
›Reuse approved templates and design systems, and avoid uploading unnecessary files.
›Refine and reuse an existing project instead of regenerating a new one from scratch.
›Request a higher monthly usage or spending limit through the intranet form when business needs justify it.
If you need more capacity
›For business work that needs more capacity, request a higher monthly usage or spending limit through the intranet form →
›Higher monthly usage or spending limits require cost-approver approval.
›The monthly spending limit should be visible in your Claude account.
Not allowed
Do not move Sandvik work into private accounts or unapproved tools to avoid limits or cost.
